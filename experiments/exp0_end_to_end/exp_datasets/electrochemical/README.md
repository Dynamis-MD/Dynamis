- Pt degree: d = 54 (only 0.51× water degree of 103)

- Z-profile shows sharp Pt-water boundary + water layering at interface
- Worst I2 of all system types (2.4–2.6 at k=64)

## On the Necessity of Large-Scale Electrochemical Simulations

Electrochemical interfaces are non-uniform along z but **uniform in x-y** (periodic surface). A small x-y cell (30×30 Å) with PBC already captures the full physics for a flat, homogeneous surface. Large-scale distributed simulation is NOT needed for this case.

Large x-y IS needed only when the surface itself is heterogeneous:
- Surface defects (steps, vacancies): defect spacing > PBC period
- Nanoparticle catalysts: 3D particle, cannot use x-y PBC
- Alloy/grain boundary surfaces: compositional variation in x-y
- Correlated multi-ion adsorption: ion-ion spacing > PBC

**For this work**: the Pt+water systems serve as a **benchmark for 1D layered non-uniformity**, demonstrating the strongest imbalance (I2 = 2.6) among all tested system types. They complement the 3D localized non-uniformity of solvated proteins. We do not claim that flat Pt(111)+water specifically requires distributed computing.

## No Public Large-Scale Datasets Available

Public metal-solvent datasets are all DFT-scale (<300 atoms):
- **OC25** (Meta, 2025): avg 144 atoms, catalyst + solvent, 88 elements — https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.17862
- **ElectroFace**: ~200 atoms, Pt/Au/Cu + water AIMD — https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-025-05338-5

Large-scale NNP-MD of electrochemical interfaces exists but data is not public:
- **Fujitsu** (2025): 100k atoms, all-solid-state battery interface — https://global.fujitsu/en-global/pr/news/2025/12/01-01
- **OTF-MLMD Li battery** (2025): 300k atoms, Li + electrolyte SEI — https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cej.2025.161234
- **Allegro Pt NP** (2024): 2nm Pt nanoparticle on SiO₂ — https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00720v1

Self-construction using ASE is the only viable approach for partition benchmarking at this scale.
